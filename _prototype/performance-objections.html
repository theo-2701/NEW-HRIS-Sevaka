Objections

Three surfaces for three roles. An employee objects to a signed score or to a final rejection and watches their own case; the active holder — the assessor, the next supervisor up, or HR once the chain runs out — reads what has been laid on them and answers; HR watches everything still unanswered company-wide and can reopen a closed objection window. There is no client-side update or delete on an objection: answering it flips the status inside the same transaction.

Two entry points, not one form with a dropdown

Objecting to a signed score and objecting to a final rejection are separate creation screens on purpose. The first only accepts a sheet that is already approved and never carries an approval round; the second must name a round that actually carries the final-rejection outcome, and its answerer is the approver who rejected it — not the assessor.

The button is not the gate

For a supervisor the holder is forced server-side to the caller's own identity, so the grid can never be pointed at somebody else's queue. Only the currently active holder may answer: a holder who has already passed the case up the chain is refused even if the Answer button was visible a moment earlier. Answering is one-to-one — an answered objection is opened read-only, never answered twice.

Holder context reads PENILAI_ASLI while the original assessor still owes the answer, NAIK_RANTAI once it has moved up the chain, and PENADAH_HR when HR is the last catcher. Only the first appears in this dataset.

Company-wide, no holder filter, and only SUBMITTED rows — HR reads here, it never answers. An answered objection is not deleted: it simply belongs to the sheet history instead of this watchlist. Days waiting is the aging derived on the pending-objections report, quoted here so HR can see which case has sat longest.

Escalation happens off-screen

Ownership moves from the original assessor up the chain and finally to HR through a background job — there is no screen and no user action for it. The chain stops at HR: if HR sits on it, the objection stays SUBMITTED indefinitely and is only visible on the monitor board.
