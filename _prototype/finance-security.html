Place versus release — a deliberate asymmetry

Why placing a dispute hold asks for no reason while releasing one always does.

AspectPlaceRelease
Reason requiredNo — there is no reason field in the form or the payloadYes — a single free-text release note, empty is refused
Who may actFinance officer · HR manager · super adminExactly the same, including across roles — the releaser need not be the person who placed it
NotificationNone requiredWhen a finance officer releases, the HR manager is informed — not asked to approve
WhyPlacing a hold is protective and low risk; demanding a reason would only slow the response to a fresh disputeReleasing reopens the row to the normal retention sweep — a consequential decision that must leave an auditable reason

Separation of duties — opener is not auditor

Two roles deliberately split over one class of data: the health attachment of a receipt.

RoleCapabilityExplicit prohibition
Opener — health data officer (also HR manager)Opens the content of one receipt attachment. Every successful open writes a trail row before the result is returned; a refused attempt writes nothing.Given no endpoint to review the trail — the role opens, it does not audit.
Auditor — HR manager (also super admin)Reviews who has opened what, on this screen.Cannot open attachment content from here; this board shows metadata only, never the document.

The finance officer is refused on both counts (403) even though they own the disbursement of the very same claim. The HR manager intersects both roles — this is functional separation, not a full maker-checker split.
