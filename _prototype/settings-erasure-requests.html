Personal Data Erasure Requests

Records one erasure request from a data subject — usually a former employee with no account — and then follows how far each depositary service has got with it. The only insert-and-read story in the settings service: the actor is a person, not a sweep, and the request arrives by letter and is written down by HR.

The list answers "which one needs looking at", so it carries counts rather than the tracker rows themselves — the detail answers the rest. Paging is mandatory from birth: the set only grows, and no row is ever deleted.
No name or NIK is stored against a request — only the subject's identifier. Name and NIK are exchanged at render time from the authorisation database over a read-only, three-column grant (id · nama · nik). Nothing is copied back. If the exchange fails the list still stands with its statuses and dates and only the name column goes blank — compliance evidence does not depend on it. A subject already anonymised correctly reads ANONYMIZED: the right answer, not a failure.
🔴 There is no "mark done" button and no action column on either screen — the subject’s name is the link into the detail, nothing more. Status only ever rises by machine: the executing service marks its own tracker row through its own machine identity, and the parent request is lifted to COMPLETED by the next sweep — at most one sweep interval later, never the instant the last depositary finishes.

🔴 The broadcast count must be on screen. A row that stays PENDING sweep after sweep produces no error anywhere — that counter is the only number that makes the situation visible. settings-service itself sitting at 12 broadcasts and still PENDING is exactly the case it exists to expose.
Legal transitions are PENDING↔HELD, either into COMPLETED, and COMPLETED into itself (idempotent). Leaving COMPLETED is refused forever — anonymisation cannot be undone. No reason-for-hold field is accepted: why a service holds lives in that service's own schema.
